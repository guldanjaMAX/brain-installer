# Before we build: the questions your Brain has to answer

This is the first thing we do together, and it is the only part where I need
real thought from you rather than access or admin.

It is not a form about your files. It works the other way around. You tell me
what you want to be able to ask, and the answers tell us both what has to be in
there. Every question you write becomes a test your Brain has to pass before I
hand it over.

---

## How to run this

Paste this whole document into Claude or ChatGPT and say **"interview me through
this, one question at a time."** It will ask, wait, and keep your answers
together. Then send me what it produces.

Three rules make it work:

1. **One question at a time.** Do not read ahead and batch it.
2. **"Gap" is a real answer.** If you do not know where something lives, say
   gap and move on. A recorded gap is worth more to me than a guess, because I
   will go looking for it. A guess I will believe.
3. **Stop whenever.** Save what you have and come back. It is meant to be picked
   up in pieces.

There is no time limit and no prize for speed. Most people take somewhere
between forty minutes and two sittings.

## Part 1. First, which are you?

**1.1** Is this Brain mainly for **a business** or **a household**?

Either is fine, and plenty of people are both. Pick the one that feels like the
centre of gravity. If it is a business that owns your house, say business. If it
is your life that happens to include an LLC or two, say household.

> Everything below adapts to that answer. Skip the part that does not apply.

**If business:**
- **1.2** What entities exist, and what does each one do?
- **1.3** When does your fiscal year end?
- **1.4** For each of these, what system holds it: payroll, bills you pay,
  banking, customers, contracts and documents.
- **1.5** Who else touches the books? A bookkeeper, a controller, an accountant.
  Name them even though you are the one answering. I am not asking them
  anything; I need to know they exist.

**If household:**
- **1.2** What sits under it: accounts, taxes, legal, insurance, investments,
  property, estate. Which of those are actually messy?
- **1.3** Are there entities attached? An LLC, a trust, a rental.
- **1.4** Who else is involved in the money? A spouse, an accountant, an
  attorney, an adult child.

## Part 2. The twenty questions

This is the important part. After install, we sit down and lock in twenty
questions your Brain has to answer correctly. That set becomes its acceptance
test, and it is the difference between a Brain that impresses you in a demo and
one you actually trust in month four.

Writing them now, before anything is connected, does two things. It tells me
what has to be loaded. And it stops the questions being quietly shaped by
whatever we happen to find, which would make the test meaningless.

**For every question below, I need two things: the question, and where you
think the answer lives.** The second half is the part that does the work.

### 2.5 Five things it must NOT answer

The most important five, and the least intuitive.

## Part 3. What must be in there

Now the other direction, and it is quick because Part 2 did most of the work.

**3.1 The five documents.** Beyond what you already named, what are the five
documents that absolutely have to be findable? Not categories. Specific things
you could point at.

**3.2 The newest thing.** What is the most recent thing you filed, and roughly
when? I use this to check the load did not quietly stop early.

**3.3 The oldest thing that still matters.** What year, and what is it? This
catches the case where everything before a move or a migration is invisible and
nobody notices.

**3.4 What is a picture.** What in here is scanned, photographed, or
handwritten?

> Worth being straight with you: there is no text recognition today. A scanned
> contract is a picture of a contract to this system. It gets stored and it does
> not get read. I would rather you know that now than discover it in week three
> when a contract question comes back empty.

**3.5 What it is called.** What do you call things internally that an outsider
would not recognise? Job names, client nicknames, project codes, the shorthand
in your file names.

> This one punches above its weight. A Brain that does not know "the Henderson
> job" means "Project 4412" will fail a question it has everything it needs to
> answer.

## Part 4. What must never be in there

As important as what goes in, and cheaper to decide now than to remove later.

**4.1** Which folders, drives, mailboxes or accounts are off limits? Personal,
medical, legal, HR, anything.

**4.2** Anything covered by someone else's confidentiality? Client files under
NDA, patient records, applicant data.

**4.3 For each area you just named, give me one distinctive word or phrase that
would only ever appear in it.** A surname, a matter number, a project codename.

> I use these as tripwires. After loading, I search your Brain for each one. If
> any of them surfaces, something got in that should not have, and we find out
> before you do rather than after.

---

## Part 5. Where it all lives

Most of this you have already answered in Part 2 without noticing. This is the
sweep-up.

**5.1** Naming everything from Part 2, where does it actually live? Google
Drive, email, a CRM, accounting software, Dropbox, a filing cabinet.

**5.2** For each: whose account owns it? Yours, or someone else's?

**5.3** Is any of it in an account belonging to someone who has left, or in a
shared drive rather than your own?

> This is the single most common reason a load comes back smaller than expected,
> and it is nothing to do with memory. It is access.

**5.4** Is there one folder or mailbox that, on its own, would already be worth
it?

## Part 6. Who else should be able to ask it

**6.1** Besides you, who should be able to use this? A spouse, a business
partner, a bookkeeper, an accountant, an attorney.

**6.2** For each one, should they see **everything**, or only part of it?

> Be straight with me here, because it changes what I can honestly promise you.
> As built today the Brain has one level of access: anyone who can ask it
> questions can reach anything it has read. Per-person limits are being built
> and are not finished.
>
> If everyone on your list should see everything, we are fine and we proceed.
>
> If your bookkeeper should not see the household, or a partner should not see
> the other entity, tell me now. I will tell you honestly whether to wait, and I
> would rather do that than take your money and explain it in week three.

## What happens with this

I turn your answers into two things.

**A checklist of what should be in there**, which I check the finished load
against, item by item, and show you the result. Your five documents, your date
range, your tripwires. If something you named is not findable, that is a
failure and I fix it before handover, not after.

**A first draft of your twenty questions**, which we finish together against the
live Brain once it is loaded. You confirm each answer's evidence yourself. That
session is the acceptance test, and its result is what tells you whether this
thing is ready.

If you answered "gap" anywhere, that is not a problem. It is a list of what to
go find, and it is the most useful thing this document produces.
